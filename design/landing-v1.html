The problem

Gross settlement ties up capital.

In a network of trading partners, most obligations cancel each other out. Yet traditional rails settle every leg in full — multiplying both the liquidity locked and the number of on-chain transactions.

Gross settlement
Every invoice moves in full
  • A pays B 100, B pays C 100, C pays A 80
  • 3 on-chain transfers, 280 USDC of float
  • Capital sits idle between legs
  • Each transfer pays gas and slippage
280 USDC moved
Multilateral netting
Only the residual moves
  • Map obligations across all counterparties
  • Net off-chain: A net −20, B net 0, C net +20
  • Settle one atomic transaction on Arc
  • Capital efficiency approaches theoretical max
20 USDC moved · 92.86% saved
How it works

Three steps to atomic settlement.

1
Register & submit
Enterprises register on-chain, then submit bilateral obligations (invoices) with an amount, counterparty, and due date.
submitObligation(
  creditor: 0x95eF…,
  amount: 100 USDC,
  dueDate: 2026-07-25
)
2
Confirm & close cycle
Counterparties confirm each obligation bilaterally. The operator snapshots confirmed items into a netting cycle.
closeCycle([0, 1, 2])
// locks 3 obligations
3
Net & settle
Off-chain engine computes minimum transfers. One atomic settle() moves residuals in USDC on Arc.
settle(
  payers: [Alice],
  receivers: [Carol]
)
// net: 0.2 USDC moved
